Memory care tours happen in the middle of the day, when the dining room is bright, an activity is running, and the staff-to-resident picture looks reassuring. That picture is real — for that hour. The number that actually protects your parent is the one nobody volunteers: how many awake caregivers are on the floor overnight.

## The license is a floor, not a promise

Every licensed community must meet a minimum staffing standard. That standard tells you the least they are allowed to do, not what you will get on a Tuesday night. It says nothing about how many of those caregivers are agency floats who have never met your parent, or what happens when two residents need help at the same moment.

## Why the overnight number matters most

In dementia care, the highest-risk hours are the ones with the fewest visitors and the least light. Sundowning, wandering, and falls cluster in the evening and overnight. A unit that is generously staffed at lunch and thin at midnight has its resources pointed at the wrong end of the day.

When you ask for the real schedule and the real ratio, watch how the answer arrives. A community that tracks this proudly will hand you the numbers. One that deflects, rounds up, or redirects you to the brochure has told you something too — just not the thing they meant to.
